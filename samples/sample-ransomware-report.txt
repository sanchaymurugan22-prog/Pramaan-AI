INCIDENT REPORT: RANSOMWARE CAMPAIGN AFFECTING HOSPITAL NETWORKS
Prepared by: Regional Cyber Coordination Cell (fictional)
Report date: 30 September 2026
Classification: SAMPLE – FICTIONAL

1. Summary

A ransomware group calling itself "NightLedger" is encrypting patient-record and billing systems at
hospitals. The attackers enter through an unpatched remote-access gateway, tracked here as
CVE-2026-XXXXX (sample). The gateway flaw was first exploited on 22 September 2026. As of 28
September, 42 hospitals in five states have reported disruption, mostly to patient-record and
billing systems. Emergency care has continued at all affected hospitals, but 11 hospitals moved
to paper records for more than 48 hours.

2. How the attack works

The attackers scan the internet for remote-access gateways that have not received the vendor's
September security update. Once inside, they steal administrator passwords, switch off antivirus
tools and spread to file servers over the weekend, when fewer staff are watching. Before
encrypting files, they copy data out of the network. In three cases, backups kept on the same
network were also encrypted, which made recovery much slower.

A ransom note named "READ_ME_NIGHTLEDGER.txt" is left in every encrypted folder. The note demands
payment in cryptocurrency within 72 hours and threatens to publish stolen patient data.

Hospitals are attractive targets because their systems must stay available around the clock, and
many run older software that is hard to update without stopping services. Several affected
hospitals shared one IT service provider, which let the attackers reach more than one hospital
with the same stolen passwords.

3. Timeline

- 22 September 2026: first known exploitation of the gateway flaw.
- 24 September 2026: first hospital reports encrypted billing servers.
- 26 September 2026: the vendor publishes an emergency patch for the gateway.
- 28 September 2026: 42 hospitals in five states have reported disruption.
- 30 September 2026: this report is issued.

4. Impact

About 1.2 million patient records may have been copied. Average recovery time for affected
hospitals has been 4 days. Hospitals that had offline backups restored their systems in under
2 days. No patient deaths have been linked to the incident.

6. Recommended actions

1. Apply the vendor's September patch to every remote-access gateway today.
2. Block the two listed addresses at the network edge and review connection logs since
   22 September.
3. Reset administrator passwords and turn on two-step login for all remote access.
4. Keep at least one backup offline and test that one restore works end to end.
5. Report any sign of infection to the national cyber incident response team within 6 hours.
6. Do not pay the ransom. Payment does not guarantee that data will be returned or deleted.

7. Who should act

Hospital IT teams, state health department IT cells, and any organisation that uses the same
remote-access gateway. Senior hospital administrators should make sure patches and offline
backups are in place this week.
